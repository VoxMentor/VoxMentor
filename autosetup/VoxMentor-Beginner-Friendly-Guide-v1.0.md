## Part 1: The Basics

### 1. What Is VoxMentor?

**VoxMentor is an AI-powered platform that helps college students prepare for job interviews.** You paste a job description (like "Amazon is hiring a Software Developer"), and the AI tells you exactly what skills you need, what you are missing, and gives you mock interviews — both technical (coding) and HR (behavioral) — until you are ready to apply.

Think of it like this: you have a personal AI mentor that knows what Amazon, Google, or TCS looks for in interviews. This mentor checks what you know, creates a study plan, gives you practice problems, conducts fake interviews (where the AI talks to you like a real interviewer), and tells you exactly when you are ready to apply.

> **Simple analogy**: Imagine you want to pass a driving test. Instead of just reading the rulebook, you have a driving instructor who: (1) checks what you already know, (2) tells you what to practice, (3) gives you driving lessons, (4) gives you a mock driving test, and (5) tells you "you are ready, go take the real test." VoxMentor does exactly this — but for job interviews.

### 1.1 Why Does This Exist?

India produces 1.5 million engineering graduates every year. Only 15% get jobs through campus placements. The remaining 85% — that is over 1.2 million students — are on their own. They apply to hundreds of jobs, hear back from very few, and fail interviews they could have passed if they had prepared properly.

The problem is not that these students are not smart. The problem is that they do not know:

- **What the company actually wants** (the job description is full of jargon)
- **What they themselves do not know** (they think they are good at DSA, but they have never been tested)
- **How to practice interviews** (you cannot just read — you must do mock interviews)
- **When they are ready** (there is no score that says "you are 85% ready for Amazon")

VoxMentor solves all four problems. You paste a job description, and the AI handles the rest.

## 2. How It Works — The Simple Version

Here is the entire VoxMentor experience in 6 simple steps:

### Step 1: Paste Your Job Description

You find a job you want (e.g., "Amazon SDE-1"). You copy the job description text and paste it into VoxMentor. That is it. The AI does the rest.

### Step 2: AI Analyzes the Job

The AI reads the job description and figures out: Amazon wants Dynamic Programming (35% of interview), Graphs (25%), Arrays (15%), System Design (15%), and Trees (10%). It also figures out what HR qualities Amazon looks for (Leadership, Ownership, Customer Obsession).

### Step 3: Take a Quick Test

The AI gives you 10 quick problems across different topics to see what you already know. After the test, it says: "You are 42% ready. Your biggest gap is Dynamic Programming — you scored only 15% on DP, but Amazon asks it 35% of the time."

### Step 4: Follow Your Personal Study Plan

The AI creates a week-by-week plan: Week 1-2: DP. Week 3: Graphs. Week 4: System Design. Week 5-6: Mock interviews. Each day, it gives you practice problems at your level — not too easy, not too hard. Every student gets different problems.

### Step 5: Take Mock Interviews

When you are ready, you take a mock interview. The AI acts as the interviewer. It talks to you (voice-based, just like a real interview), gives you coding problems, asks follow-up questions, and even conducts an HR interview (behavioral questions, STAR method).

### Step 6: Get Your Readiness Score

After each mock interview, the AI updates your Readiness Score: "You are now 68% ready. +6% improvement. Practice 3 more DP sessions to reach 85%." When your score hits 85%+, the AI says: "You are ready. Apply now." You also get a resume check — the AI scans your resume against the job description and tells you what keywords to add.

> **The whole point**: You start at 42% ready. Through practice problems, AI coaching, and mock interviews, you climb to 85%+. Then you apply with confidence. That is VoxMentor in one sentence: **Paste your JD, practice, mock-interview, get ready, get the job.**

## Part 3: Under the Hood

### 4. How It Works Under the Hood — Explained Simply

This section is for people who want to understand the technology behind VoxMentor — whether you are a student, a teacher, or a developer. Everything is explained in simple terms.

#### 4.1 The Big Picture

VoxMentor has 4 main parts:

**1. The Main App (Core API)**
This is the brain. It handles everything fast — login, choosing the next problem, tracking your score, saving your progress. Built with .NET 8 (Microsoft's framework).

**2. AI Tutor Service**
This is the tutor. When you ask the AI Coach a question, this service retrieves the relevant explanation from interview prep books and streams the answer back. It is separate because LLM responses take 3-8 seconds and should not slow down the main app.

**3. Code Execution Service**
This is the code runner. When you write code and click "Run," this service executes your code in a safe sandbox (like a locked box — your code cannot escape or harm the server). It also runs plagiarism detection and AI code evaluation.

**4. Voice Service**
This handles voice. When you take a mock interview, the AI interviewer speaks to you and you speak back. This service converts your speech to text (using Whisper) and the AI's text response to speech (using Piper).

> **Why are these separated?** Imagine a restaurant. The waiter (Main App) takes orders and serves food — fast. The kitchen (AI Tutor) cooks — takes time. The dishwasher (Code Execution) cleans — separate process. The drive-through speaker (Voice Service) handles voice — different equipment. Each part runs independently so if one breaks (like the AI tutor crashing), the rest of the restaurant still works.

#### 4.4 How BKT Works — The Mastery Engine (Simplified)

BKT (Bayesian Knowledge Tracing) is the algorithm that tracks whether you have truly mastered a topic. Here is how it works in simple terms:

Every time you answer a question, the system updates its belief about whether you have mastered that topic. It does this using 4 numbers for each topic:

| Parameter | What It Means (Simple) |
|---|---|
| **Initial Knowledge** | Before you answer anything, what is the probability you already know this? Usually 10% — the system assumes you do not know it yet. |
| **Learn Rate** | How likely is it that you learn the topic from answering one question? Usually 10% per question. |
| **Slip Rate** | How likely is it that you get a question wrong even though you actually know the topic? Usually 10% — you made a careless mistake. |
| **Guess Rate** | How likely is it that you get a question right even though you do not know the topic? For a 4-option MCQ, it is 25% — you got lucky. |

When you answer correctly, mastery goes up. When you answer wrong, mastery goes down. But it is smarter than just counting correct answers — it accounts for guesses and slips. This is the same algorithm Khan Academy uses internally.

## Part 4: Your Journey

### 5. Your Complete Journey — Week by Week

Here is what a typical student experiences over 6 weeks of using VoxMentor to prepare for an Amazon interview.

**Day 0**: You create an account. You paste the Amazon SDE-1 job description. The AI analyzes it and says: "Amazon wants DP (35%), Graphs (25%), System Design (15%). You are at 42% readiness. Your biggest gap is DP."

**Week 1-2**: You practice DP problems every day. The AI gives you problems at your level — not too easy, not too hard. Every problem is unique (isomorphic — your friend cannot copy). You write code in the browser editor, the AI evaluates it. Stuck at 2 AM? You ask the AI Coach — it explains using Cracking the Coding Interview. Your DP mastery climbs from 15% to 55%.

**Week 3**: You start Graphs practice. DP mastery is 55% but the AI reminds you to review old DP problems (spaced repetition — you have not practiced DP in 4 days, it is decaying). You do 2 DP reviews + 3 new Graph problems per day. Graphs mastery: 20% → 45%. Overall readiness: 42% → 58%.

**Week 4**: System Design practice begins. You upload your resume — the AI says "72% match with JD. Add Python. Remove the table in education section." You fix your resume. Readiness: 58% → 65%.

**Week 5**: First mock interview (Technical). The AI talks to you via voice: "Hi, I am your Amazon interviewer. I will give you 2 coding problems and 1 system design question. 45 minutes." You solve 1 of 2 coding problems. The AI gives feedback: "Your DP is weak — you got the recurrence wrong. Your code is O(n²) but optimal is O(n). Practice these 5 DP problems." Readiness: 65% → 68%.

**Week 5**: Second mock interview (HR). The AI asks: "Tell me about yourself." "Describe a time you showed Ownership — use the STAR method." You answer. The AI scores: "Your STAR answer had no measurable Result. Instead of saying 'the project improved,' say 'the project improved by 30%, saving Rs. 2 lakhs.'" Readiness: 68% → 72%.

**Week 6**: Third mock interview (combined). You solve both coding problems. Your code passes all test cases. Your HR answers use STAR properly. The AI says: "You are now 85% ready. Apply now." You apply to Amazon. You crack the interview. You get the job.

## Part 5: FAQ

### 6. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need to install anything?**

A: No. VoxMentor runs in your browser. You just need a laptop with Chrome/Firefox and a microphone (for voice mock interviews). No app to install.

**Q: Is it free?**

A: Yes. The free tier gives you 1 JD analysis, 20 practice problems per day, 1 mock interview per week, and 10 AI Coach questions per hour. A Pro tier (Rs. 299/month) removes all limits and adds voice mock interviews.

**Q: What programming languages are supported?**

A: Python, Java, C++, JavaScript, and 55+ more languages. The code execution sandbox (Judge0) supports all of them.

**Q: How does the AI know what Amazon asks?**

A: The AI does not memorize specific questions. It analyzes the job description and figures out which topics Amazon weights most heavily (DP, Graphs, etc.). Then it generates problems at those topics at the right difficulty. Every student gets different problems.

**Q: Is the AI interviewer realistic?**

A: The AI uses a state machine (like a flowchart) to manage the interview: greeting → problem 1 → follow-up questions → problem 2 → system design → closing. It reacts to what you say. If you solve quickly, it gives a harder problem. If you struggle, it gives hints. It is not a real human, but it is close enough for practice.

**Q: Can the AI make mistakes?**

A: The AI Coach (Feature 7) uses RAG — it only answers from real interview prep books. It cannot make things up. If it does not know, it says "I do not have enough information." The AI interviewer and code evaluator can make occasional mistakes, but the teacher/admin can always override AI evaluations.

**Q: How is this different from LeetCode?**

A: LeetCode gives you problems. VoxMentor gives you: (1) JD analysis (what does Amazon want?), (2) Readiness Score (am I ready?), (3) mock interviews (voice + code), (4) AI evaluation (complexity, style, edge cases), (5) resume ATS check. LeetCode is a problem bank. VoxMentor is a complete interview prep platform.

**Q: How is this different from ChatGPT?**

A: ChatGPT does not know what you know (no BKT mastery tracking), does not know what Amazon wants (no JD analysis), does not run your code (no Judge0), does not conduct structured interviews (no state machine), and costs $20/month. VoxMentor is free, JD-driven, mastery-aware, code-executing, and conducts realistic mock interviews.

**Q: What if I do not have a microphone?**

A: Mock interviews work in text mode too. You type instead of speaking. Voice is preferred (real interviews are voice), but text is supported.

**Q: What data does VoxMentor store about me?**

A: Your profile (name, email, target company), mastery scores per topic, code submissions, mock interview transcripts, and resume. All stored in PostgreSQL. No data is shared with third parties. You can delete your account and all data anytime.
